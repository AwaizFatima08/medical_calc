# Ship bedside formulas first, defer dosing tools

The HomiLabs app should launch with about **25 Doctor-mode tools and 10 Public-mode tools**. All of them are simple published formulas or bedside scores that need no lab or only a cheap one, and the people using them can check the result. Patient-specific drug dosing (insulin, children's syrups, chemotherapy) should wait until a later phase, behind extra review. Doctor mode should open on renal function (Cockcroft-Gault, CKD-EPI 2021), mean arterial pressure, CHA2DS2-VA, Wells/PERC, CURB-65/CRB-65, GCS and NEWS2, which are the tools clinicians use most and that guidelines embed. Pakistani additions should follow immediately: APRI/FIB-4 for hepatitis C, the IDF-DAR Ramadan risk score, dengue warning signs and fluids, and WHO growth z-scores. Public mode should lead with Asian-cut-off BMI and waist, a South Asian diabetes risk score, home blood pressure and glucose interpreters, and the WHO non-laboratory heart-risk chart. The reason is burden: Pakistan has the **world's highest adult diabetes prevalence (31.4%)**, **40% child stunting** and the **largest number of HCV infections of any country**. The app's competitive gap is equally clear, since no Urdu, offline, Pakistan-guideline calculator app turned up, and local doctors rely on Medscape and other foreign references. Regulation favours transparent, verifiable outputs presented as reference rather than instruction. Google Play requires a specific "not a medical device" disclaimer, and MMSE and MoCA must be excluded unless licensed. Calculator apps have a documented error record, so every formula needs golden test vectors from the primary source and a recorded clinician sign-off before any code ships.

## Doctor mode: 25 bedside and cheap-lab tools form the first release

No public whole-platform usage ranking exists for MDCalc, Medscape or QxMD. Still, the evidence that does exist points to one stable core. A peer-reviewed analysis of MDCalc page views (2016–2018) found that the top stroke-related tools were **CHA2DS2-VASc, mean arterial pressure, HAS-BLED, NIHSS and CHADS2**, and reported that MDCalc reaches **more than 65% of US physicians monthly** ([JMIR Med Inform 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8369374/)). On 9 Nov 2026, MDCalc's homepage "Trending" list led with **Cockcroft-Gault creatinine clearance, weight-based levothyroxine, CKD-EPI eGFR, MAP and SpO2/FiO2**, although MDCalc labels these as featured tools rather than a strict ranking ([MDCalc](https://www.mdcalc.com/)). UK GPs grew more familiar with QRISK, ABCD2, CHADS-VASc, HAS-BLED and Wells DVT/PE between 2014 and 2020. Use of Wells PE rose 43.6% and CHADS 54.6% over that period ([BJGP Open 2020](https://doaj.org/article/717f39939c9f4fa388402a1ebd7d4659)). A 2024 US study found that physicians use calculators that are easy to reach, have simple inputs and are trusted, and that residents use them more than attendings ([Med Decis Making 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39377500/)). House officers and postgraduate trainees are therefore the natural core audience in Pakistan. Medscape is the most used named app among Pakistani medical students, at 29.3% in a 2023 CMH Lahore survey, and 54% of AKU clinical-year students use apps to look up disease criteria ([DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/e2d2743f249341ee91268517188b3f31); [PMC10413307](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10413307/)).

Usage data stop at the top tier. Below it, the ranking is expert judgement adjusted for Pakistan's case mix and limited labs. Several guideline changes in 2023–2025 decide which *version* to ship. The ESC 2024 AF guideline dropped the female-sex point, making the score **CHA2DS2-VA**. The ACC/AHA 2023 guideline still uses CHA2DS2-VASc, so the app should offer both with a toggle and default to VA ([MDedge](https://mdedge.com/cardiology/article/271567/arrhythmias-ep/goodbye-chadsvasc-sex-complicates-stroke-risk-scoring-af)). The **2021 CKD-EPI equation is race-free**, and KDIGO 2024 recommends creatinine equations first, with cystatin C added where those are unreliable ([Tufts CKD-EPI](https://www.tuftsmedicine.org/research-clinical-trials/research-institutes-research-specialty/chronic-kidney-disease-epidemiology-collaboration-ckd-epi/gfr-estimation/estimating-equations); [CCLM 2024](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/cclm-2024-1082/html)). The Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2021 makes a **strong recommendation against qSOFA as a sole screening tool** compared with NEWS, MEWS or SIRS. qSOFA should therefore appear only with that caveat, and NEWS2 should sit next to it ([SSC 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/8486643/)). CURB-65 and CRB-65 have local evidence: a prospective Aga Khan University study found that **CURB-65 classed 46% of CAP patients as low risk**, enough to identify patients who could be discharged from the ER ([AKU Scholars](https://scholars.aku.edu/en/publications/prospective-comparison-of-prediction-rules-of-mortality-risk-for-)). CRB-65 needs no urea, which matters where labs are slow.

The cardiovascular-risk choice is the most consequential version decision. The 2025 AHA/ACC blood pressure guideline replaced the Pooled Cohort Equations with **PREVENT**, which starts drug therapy for stage 1 hypertension at a **10-year risk of 7.5% or more** ([ACC 2025](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2025/10/01/01/new-in-clinical-guidance-hbp)). PREVENT was derived in US cohorts and asks for UACR, HbA1c and a US social-deprivation index. In Pakistan those inputs are often missing or meaningless. The **WHO 2019 regional charts** are the better default. Their non-laboratory version classifies people almost identically to the lab version. In rural India it showed **86.5% sensitivity and 90.3% specificity** against the lab chart at a 5% cut-off ([PMC8925162](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8925162/); [PMC9438460](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9438460)). One open question must be settled before coding: the older WHO/ISH charts put Pakistan in the EMR-D sub-region rather than South Asia, so the developer has to confirm which 2019 region chart applies ([WHO/ISH, NCBI Bookshelf](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK148602/bin/simplifiedtools-m2.pdf)). Agreement between models is not guaranteed in South Asians either; in Sri Lankans, Framingham and WHO/ISH agreed poorly ([Authorea](https://authorea.com/doi/full/10.22541/au.159318492.20249418)).

The table below gives the recommended phasing. Phase 1 is the MVP. Phase 2 follows once Phase 1 has passed validation. Phase 3 holds high-input ICU tools, newer scores not yet validated outside their derivation setting, and anything that produces a drug dose.

ABCD2 needs a note: NICE's advice against it for TIA triage comes from memory in the research notes and was not re-verified, so check NICE NG128 before labelling it. Older scores should not disappear silently. Pakistani local protocols and FCPS/MRCP syllabi still cite CHA2DS2-VASc, MELD-Na and ASCVD. Each superseded tool therefore belongs as a toggle inside its successor, with a "superseded by X (year)" chip. The other replacements in this group are **MELD 3.0** (OPTN, July 2023), the **Phoenix Sepsis Score** (2024), which replaced SIRS-based paediatric criteria, and **SOFA-2**, published in JAMA in October 2025 with an ICU-mortality AUROC of 0.79 against 0.77 for the original SOFA ([PMC10363200](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10363200); [SCCM](https://sccm.org/blog/sccm-task-force-develops-new-criteria-to-identify-pediatric-sepsis); [PubMed 41159833](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41159833/)). Phoenix belongs in a Pakistan app despite its input load: in lower-resource settings, in-hospital mortality among children meeting the criteria was **28.5%, against 7.1% in higher-resource settings**.

## Public mode: ten screening tools, with all medication dosing left out

The single proven consumer favourite is BMI. The leading Android BMI app shows **10,000,000+ installs** on Google Play and an estimated 24 million downloads in total ([AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/bmi-calculator/com.appovo.bmicalculator)). No dataset ranks other consumer calculators against each other, so the rest of the Public list is weighted by Pakistan's burden and by how safely a layperson can act on the result. For diabetes, the strongest choice is the **MDRF Indian Diabetes Risk Score (IDRS)**. It has four items (age, waist, physical activity, family history) and needs no lab, and it has repeated South Asian validations: in Jaipur, a score above 60 gave **62.2% sensitivity and 73.7% specificity** ([PubMed 21121209](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21121209)), and Mangalore found 60 to be the best-balanced cut-off ([IJCMPH](https://www.ijcmph.com/index.php/ijcmph/article/download/3831/2617/15164)). FINDRISC has been used in screening in Rawalpindi and Karachi, but neither score has been formally validated in Pakistan ([PMC12266448](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12266448/)). The app should therefore call the result a screening estimate that prompts a blood test.

Blood pressure interpretation should follow the 2025 AHA/ACC categories: normal below 120/80, elevated 120–129/<80, stage 1 at 130–139 or 80–89, and stage 2 at 140/90 or above. **Severe hypertension above 180/120** is a red flag, and becomes a hypertensive emergency when organ damage is present ([AHA 2025](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/2025-high-blood-pressure-guideline\top-things-to-know)). The same guideline **does not endorse cuffless watch or ring readings** for BP assessment, so the app should label wearable readings as wellness data. Pakistan has its own Pakistan Hypertension League guideline (4th edition, JPMA supplement, August 2023). Its thresholds were not extracted in this research, and they must be read and reconciled with AHA/ACC before the BP tool ships ([JPMA issue](https://jpma.org.pk/index.php/public_html/issue/view/57/8)). For glucose, the **ADAG formula, eAG (mg/dL) = 28.7 × HbA1c − 46.7**, converts HbA1c to an average glucose: 7% corresponds to about 154 mg/dL ([PMC2742903](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2742903)).

For mental health, Urdu instruments with local evidence exist, so the app needs no fresh translation. The Urdu PHQ-9 showed **94.7% sensitivity and 88.9% specificity at a cut-off of 10** in pregnant women in rural Pakistan ([PeerJ 2018](https://peerj.com/articles/5185)). The Urdu GAD-7 showed **98.6% sensitivity at a cut-off of 10** in a 2026 clinical sample ([PagePress](https://www.pagepressjournals.org/hls/article/view/14309)). The best PHQ-9 cut-off falls to 6 in coronary patients, though ([UTS OPUS](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/114867)), so results should be worded as "talk to a doctor" rather than as a diagnosis. Any non-zero answer to PHQ-9 item 9 must interrupt the flow with a crisis screen. The Pakistani helpline numbers for that screen are not yet verified.

Children's paracetamol and ibuprofen dosing is the hard case: the demand is real and so is the error record. Caregivers misdose in both directions. In Sri Lanka, **43% of children received a supra-therapeutic paracetamol dose** and only one caregiver could calculate a weight-based dose ([DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/b8c347a5a5594d219dd31f78af67fec8)). In Bangladesh in 2026, inaccuracy was highly prevalent, mostly as underdosing ([Banglajol](https://banglajol.info/index.php/CBMJ/article/view/87636)). Patient-specific dosing is also the feature most likely to turn the app into a regulated device. This tool should stay out of Public mode until a later phase, and then appear only as a weight-to-mL lookup. That version would require an explicit syrup strength, hard caps and sign-off from a paediatric pharmacist.

How results are presented matters as much as which tools are chosen. Adult literacy is about **55% (42% for women)** ([PMC8523976](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8523976)). Icon arrays improve risk understanding across numeracy levels, though they help less when someone is low in both numeracy and graph literacy ([PMC4504147](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4504147)). Every Public result should therefore be one plain Urdu sentence, a colour band carrying a text label (never colour alone), a single next action, and a red-flag list. Natural frequencies ("15 out of 100 people like you") should replace bare percentages, and Urdu audio should be available for every result. Shared family phones are common: individual ownership is 50%, while more than 70% of households use the internet ([APP](https://www.app.com.pk/?p=1140479)). The app should therefore store sensitive answers only with consent and only on the device.

## Pakistan defaults: mg/dL units, Asian cut-offs, Ramadan and hepatitis scores

Units come first, because unit mix-ups cause most calculator errors. Pakistani lab reports such as Chughtai's give **glucose and creatinine in mg/dL and HbA1c in %** ([Scribd report example](https://www.scribd.com/document/684403729/Report-Viewer)). The app should default to conventional units and put a visible SI toggle on every lab field. This serves doctors who trained in the UK or work in the Gulf without forcing them to convert by hand. No authoritative source says which eGFR equation Pakistani labs print, so the app should name its equation on every result.

Anthropometric cut-offs need one decision before coding, because the notes contain two variants. The **WHO Asia-Pacific bands** (overweight from 23, obese from 25) have been applied to Pakistan in JPMA ([JPMA 2356](https://archive.jpma.org.pk/article-details/2356)). The Pakistan Endocrine Society has used the South Asian 23 / 27.5 bands, which match the WHO 2004 public-health action points ([FoodNavigator Asia](https://www.foodnavigator-asia.com/Article/2018/05/16/Half-of-Pakistan-s-population-found-to-be-obese-and-facing-high-diabetes-risk)). Both start "at risk" at 23, so the safe choice is to flag risk at **23** and show which standard the app applied. The Lancet 2004 consultation and a clinician should settle the obesity threshold. Waist cut-offs should follow **IDF 2005 for South Asians: 90 cm for men and 80 cm for women** ([MJA 2005](https://www.mja.com.au/journal/2005/183/4/mainstreaming-metabolic-syndrome-definitive-definition)). The ADA likewise screens Asian Americans for diabetes from a BMI of 23 ([PMC4407753](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4407753)).

Ramadan is the clearest feature the app can own. The **IDF-DAR 2021 risk calculator** scores pre-fast risk as low (0–3), moderate (3.5–6) or high (above 6) ([Thieme](https://thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/html/10.1055/s-0045-1813010)). Validation supports it: hypoglycaemia during Ramadan rose from about **6% in low-risk to about 35% in high-risk patients** ([PMC9969239](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9969239)). One 2022 study called the score conservative. A reported 2026 update keeps the same bands but needs verification. The module also needs a Hijri display with a ±1-day moon-sighting offset, because Pakistan's Ruet-e-Hilal Committee decides the start of Ramadan.

Hepatitis tools rank higher in Pakistan than their MDCalc popularity suggests. Pakistan has about **8.8 million HCV infections**, the most of any country, and unsafe injections cause 44% of new infections ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2494516/pakistan)). In a Pakistani biopsy study (n=158), **APRI below 1 ruled out advanced fibrosis in about 70% and FIB-4 below 1.45 in about 74%** ([JCPSP 2009](https://jcpsp.pk/archive/2009/Aug2009/04.pdf)). A Karachi FibroScan comparison found that FIB-4 outperformed APRI, with an AUC of 0.994 against 0.866 ([TPMJ](https://theprofesional.com/index.php/tpmj/article/view/8915)). In dialysis patients APRI did better ([J Transl Intern Med 2018](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.2478/jtim-2018-0008/html)). The two FIB-4 cut-off pairs in the notes are not a contradiction: 1.45/3.25 is the HCV pair and 1.3/2.67 the MASLD pair. The tool should ask for the liver disease context and apply the matching pair.

Dengue support should follow WHO 2009 (probable dengue, then warning signs, then severe dengue). Its text should reproduce the NIH Islamabad danger signs: severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding, altered mental status, falling platelets, and early shock signs requiring referral ([NHSRC 2025](https://nhsrc.gov.pk/SiteImage/Downloads/Advisory-Dengue%20Fever-2025%20(March%202025).docx)). Confirmed cases rose from **21,016 in 2023 to 33,394 in 2025** ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1983155)). No Pakistani mL/kg fluid table was found, so the clinician fluid calculator must use the WHO handbook regimen and cite it. Maternal and child tools carry the rest of the local burden: **MMR of 155 per 100,000 live births**, driven by PPH, pre-eclampsia and sepsis ([The News](https://thenews.com.pk/amp/1298129-pakistan-among-four-nations-driving-half-of-global-maternal-deaths-in-2023-un-report)). NNS 2018 found **40.2% stunting, 17.7% wasting, and anaemia in 53.7% of under-5s and 42.7% of women** ([NNS 2018](https://www.unicef.org/pakistan/media/1871/file/KeyFindings-NationalNutritionSurvey2018.pdf); [PMC10421143](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10421143)). The Mentzer index and WHO Hb bands follow from these figures, as do MUAC bands, EDD and shock index. Offline-first is a requirement rather than a preference: internet penetration was **45.6% in 2026** ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-pakistan)).

## Licences and law: exclude MMSE and MoCA, keep every output verifiable

Copyright protects wording and layout, not methods. US law excludes "any idea, procedure, process, system, method of operation" from protection, so a published formula can be implemented freely as long as the app writes its own labels and cites the paper ([Copyright Office Review Board](https://www.Copyright.gov/rulings-filings/review-board/docs/accugrid.pdf)). Questionnaires are different, because validity depends on the exact items, and two holders enforce their rights. PAR's MMSE terms forbid reproduction in any form or language without written permission, and a 2011 PAR claim forced the takedown of a rival cognitive test ([RAPS](https://www.raps.org/resource/copyright-claim-threatens-medical-diagnostic-test.html)). MoCA requires prior written permission and a licensing agreement, and its owner sells its own official app ([MoCA permission](https://mocacognition.com/permission)). **Both must stay out of the app** unless HomiLabs obtains a written licence. Rewording them does not help.

Several instruments are usable on stated terms. The **PHQ-9 and GAD-7** need no permission to reproduce or translate ([CamCOPS](https://camcops.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tasks/phq9.html)). The **EPDS** may be reproduced if the authors and source paper are cited ([CamCOPS EPDS](https://camcops.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tasks/epds.html)). The **GCS** is free for clinical care with acknowledgement of the University of Glasgow and Sir Graham Teasdale. Its site does not address commercial software, so an ad-funded app should email the GCS team to confirm ([glasgowcomascale.org](https://glasgowcomascale.org/permissions)). **QRISK3** is LGPL and the ClinRisk disclaimer must appear next to any score. Because it needs cholesterol and has licence obligations, QRISK3 is a Phase 2 doctor option at most, even though it has a "Pakistani" ethnicity field ([CRAN QRISK3](https://archive.linux.duke.edu/cran/web/packages/QRISK3/COPYRIGHTS)). The terms for FINDRISC, IDRS, AUDIT, STOP-Bang, Braden, NIHSS, SCORE2, PREVENT and the WHO charts were not confirmed, and the terms for the Urdu validated versions were not checked. Each needs a check against the holder's site and an entry in a licence register before inclusion. CamCOPS publishes a per-instrument "intellectual property status", which is a good model for that register.

Google Play sets firm requirements. Every health app must complete the **Health apps declaration** and link a public privacy policy, both in the Console and inside the app. An app that is not a regulated device must state in its description that it is "**not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition**", and must remind users to consult a healthcare professional ([Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16679511?hl=en)). The updated policy took effect on **28 May 2025** ([Play policy preview](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13996823)). An offline app with AdMob still has to declare the ad SDK's data collection in the Data safety form. The research did not confirm Play's ad-policy and Families rules for health apps, so the app should target adults and avoid any appeal to children.

Device regulation pushes in the same direction from several countries. Under the FDA's January 2026 clinical decision support guidance, HCP-facing software whose basis a clinician can independently review stays outside device rules. Consumer-facing tools are not covered by that carve-out ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/5-key-takeaways-from-fdas-revised-clinical-decision-support-cds-software-guidance)). The UK MHRA treats easy-to-verify calculators as unlikely devices but drug- or device-specific calculators as likely ones ([EFPIA](https://www.efpia.eu/media/b4bnh2ef/efpia-position-on-medical-calculator-qualification_v5_sept2024.pdf)). The EU is stricter: MDCG 2019-11 lists "calculator for risk scores or medication doses informing the therapeutical decision" as **Class IIa** ([OpenRegulatory](https://openregulatory.com/mdcg/mdcg-2019-11)). DRAP's Medical Devices Rules 2017 include software in the device definition, but no SaMD guidance was found ([Freyr](https://www.freyrsolutions.com/fr/medical-devices/medical-device-registration-in-pakistan)). India's CDSCO finalised such guidance in July 2026, and DRAP may follow it ([CDSCO](https://www.cdsco.gov.in/opencms/export/sites/CDSCO_WEB/Pdf-documents/Guidance-document-on-Medical-Device-Software-under-MDR-2017.pdf)). Five design rules follow. Show the formula, inputs, units and citation on every result. Present bands with the source's interpretation, not instructions such as "start a statin". Keep drug-specific dosing out of the MVP. Make no diagnosis claims in Urdu or English marketing. Get legal advice on whether to geo-restrict Doctor mode in the EU.

The doctor-mode gate should be a **one-time "I am a registered healthcare professional" self-declaration**, stored locally. This keeps the app offline, account-free and outside Play's account-deletion rule. A PMDC-number check would mean collecting identifiers through a backend. That brings privacy duties and Data safety declarations, and no public PMDC verification API was found. The gate is a label, not a safety barrier, so Doctor-mode tools must be safe to show even if a layperson clicks through. This is one more reason to defer dosing tools. Pakistan still had **no enacted data protection law as of May 2026**, but GDPR applies to EU users through ads consent ([Mondaq 2026](https://www.mondaq.com/privacy-protection/1716564/why-pakistan-is-stalling-on-data-protection-a-nations-digital-dilema)). Local-only, opt-in history with a single "clear" control keeps the app's exposure small in both regimes.

## Every formula needs a golden test vector and a clinician signature

Calculator errors are rare but clinically serious. A JMIR audit of 14 apps ran 1,240 tests and found **98.6% accuracy**, but the 17 errors **clustered in Child-Pugh and MELD, and 47% of them changed prognosis** ([JMIR 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3936266)). Insulin-dose apps were worse: most offered no protection against dosing errors and some actively contributed to them ([BMC Medicine 2015](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s12916-015-0314-7.pdf)). ClinRisk lists altered coefficients, wrong inputs and poor programming as the misuse risks for QRISK3, and requires implementers to match the reference code. That standard should apply to every tool in the app.

The validation rule follows from this. No formula or threshold is coded until it has been checked against its primary source, meaning the derivation paper, the guideline text or an official calculator. The input/output definitions in the research master table are textbook knowledge and were not individually sourced, and many thresholds in the Public-mode notes are marked "not verified this session". This applies with particular force to the exact coefficients of CKD-EPI 2021, MELD 3.0, PREVENT, SOFA-2 and Phoenix, the IDRS bands, the IDF-DAR items, the PHL categories and the WHO 2019 region chart. Each calculator then needs a set of golden vectors run as Dart unit tests in CI. The vectors come from the paper's worked examples, official reference tools (qrisk.org, the AHA PREVENT tool, the `WHORiskCalculator` R package), and boundary values at every cut-off and unit conversion. MELD bounds and caps and Child-Pugh need extra cases, because those are where the 2014 errors were. Range checks should reject impossible values.

A clinician who did not write the code must review each calculator against its source and record a sign-off. Paediatric dose tools also need a paediatric pharmacist, and Urdu result text needs a Pakistani doctor plus testing with 5–8 low-literacy users, including women and rural users. Each result screen should show the citation, formula version, last-reviewed date and app build. A guideline-version register reviewed at least yearly should catch the next CHA2DS2-VA or MELD 3.0 change before users do.

## Conclusion

A Pakistan-first calculator does not win by matching MDCalc's 900 tools. It wins by doing about 35 tools without error, in mg/dL and Urdu and offline, and by adding a few that foreign apps cannot offer: IDF-DAR Ramadan risk, APRI/FIB-4 tuned to HCV, NIH dengue danger signs, and WHO growth z-scores. The same principle keeps the app inside Google Play's non-device category, clear of copyright disputes and on the safe side of FDA, MHRA and DRAP thinking: **transparent published formulas whose output a clinician can check, presented as reference bands rather than instructions**. Dosing tools are the most demanded and the most dangerous, and they are where that position ends. Keeping them out of the MVP buys time to build the formulary-backed, pharmacist-reviewed version they need.

Three unknowns could still change the plan, and each is cheap to resolve before coding starts. The first is which WHO 2019 region chart applies to Pakistan, the EMR or the South Asia version. The second is what the PHL 4th edition actually sets as BP categories. The third is whether GCS, IDRS and FINDRISC terms allow use in an ad-funded commercial app. A short survey of Pakistani house officers would also replace the expert-judgement ranking below Phase 1 with real local data. No such survey exists yet, and it would turn the app's first users into its first evidence base.
